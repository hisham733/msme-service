# D-006 — What selects the treatment: the document class, or the author?

## Ruling (MG, 2026-10-19 — §3 = A)

**The document class selects the treatment (D-004/R2 is unchanged): news → rewrite; the statement/
commentary class → near-verbatim body.** The author-driven hypothesis stays **untested**; it is
resolved only if the T-009 samples arrive or MSME answers confirm-item 0. Operationally: a future
news release is rewritten (not drafted verbatim), and a commentary release keeps the near-verbatim
body with normalised front-matter (T-008). The T-007 finding that even the near-verbatim set has an
editor-normalised title/dateline supports this reading — "near-verbatim" describes the body, not the
whole page.

## The question (MG, 2026-10-19)

MG's review of the S04 §3 ruling: the near-verbatim practice may be **author**-driven — the head editor
(Aileen Anthony) repurposes near-verbatim **whatever the class**, while commentary written by a
non-Aileen editor gets a modified title. MG proposed: **"regardless of type (news or opinion or
commentary) → verbatim or minimal change"**, tuned for both Aileen and non-Aileen editors.

## The measurement (30-pair corpus · `PUBLISHER_ACCOUNTS.md` §4b)

| account | news-class docs | commentary-class docs |
|---|---|---|
| Aileen Anthony | **none** | 5 — near-verbatim 95–98% |
| natashaisabel1006 | 19 — rewrite 2–52% | 3 — rewrite 12–41% |
| harriet.santhana | 3 — rewrite 11–27% | **none** |

**All three candidate drivers are confounded:** every near-verbatim piece is the same account (Aileen)
on the same date (12 Oct); no Aileen news-class piece exists; no non-Aileen commentary-class piece exists
after 12 Oct. The corpus cannot separate class from author from date.

## EF's reading

- **News is never verbatim in the corpus** — 22 news-class pieces across two accounts, reuse 2–67%
  (median ≈ 28). A blanket "verbatim regardless of type" would fail every measured news pair.
- **The docx does not ask for verbatim.** Its Content-Integrity rule is *"use only the press release…
  do not introduce external facts, statistics, interpretations, assumptions or conclusions"* and its
  Standard Article Flow prescribes a rewritten title, excerpt, opening, 3–4 subheaders and quotes —
  i.e. "faithful" = **no added information**, not "unchanged wording". The near-verbatim treatment is
  live practice only (the head editor), never in the docx.
- The widening (T-007) even shows Aileen's near-verbatim set is not pure verbatim: the title is
  normalised (C-018) and dateline/opening may be dropped (C-023).

## Options

- **A — keep the class trigger (D-004/R2), author as an untested hypothesis (EF-recommended).**
  News → rewrite; commentary class → near-verbatim; resolve author-vs-class by the **T-009** factorial
  test or by asking MSME (confirm-item 0). *Effect:* matches all 30 measured pairs.
- **B — MG's blanket rule: verbatim/minimal change regardless of type.** *Effect:* contradicts 22
  measured news pairs; the skill would draft near-verbatim news that the portal rewrites (and the docx
  prescribes rewriting). Would need MSME to confirm the target has changed.

## Note

EF is not asking to re-open R2 (the commentary class is near-verbatim either way); the open part is
whether **news** also flips. Blocks nothing today; it matters when the first news piece would be
drafted under the winner.
