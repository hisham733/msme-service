## Title

**Six Homegrown Chips, Including Malaysia's First FPGA, Launched at AiSEM@Selangor 2026**

## Excerpt

KUALA LUMPUR, 16 OCTOBER 2026 - Six homegrown chips designed by companies at the Malaysia
Semiconductor IC Design Park, spanning edge AI, automotive, drones and space, were launched at the
AiSEM@Selangor 2026 summit.

## Body

Six homegrown semiconductor chips designed by companies at the Malaysia Semiconductor IC Design Park
were launched today by YAB Dato' Seri Amirudin bin Shari, Dato' Menteri Besar Selangor, and Minister
of Economy YB Akmal Nasrullah Mohd Nasir at the AI Innovation Conference, AiSEM@Selangor 2026.

The chips, developed by SkyeChip, MaiStorage, Silicon X, Alphaswift Industries, Infinecs Systems and
Weeroc Space, include Malaysia's first homegrown field-programmable gate array (FPGA). They target
artificial intelligence, automotive, data centres, unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) and space.

The launch reflects the growing capability of Malaysian companies in front-end integrated circuit
(IC) design, as the country moves from "Made in Malaysia" to "Made by Malaysia".

## AI and Autonomy

SkyeChip's MARS1000 is a 7nm edge AI processor that runs AI inference locally on devices rather than
in the cloud, keeping data on-premises and responses fast. It suits real-time applications such as
autonomous robotics, smart agriculture, intelligent video analytics and AI education systems.
SkyeChip was founded in 2019 by industry veterans from Intel, Altera and Broadcom and holds 113
patents across Malaysia, the US and China.

MaiStorage's aiDAPTIV+ combines high-performance cache memory SSDs with memory management middleware
so that a system's GPU, system and cache memory work together, letting organisations run larger AI
models on their own systems without adding more GPUs and keeping their data private. It was jointly
developed by Phison Electronics Corporation and MaiStorage, Phison's regional hub and a Malaysian IC
startup founded in 2024 that designs NAND controllers, ICs and storage modules for data centres, AI
and automotive.

Alphaswift Industries' FC Mini is a compact flight controller that acts as a drone's flight brain,
handling stable flight control, real-time sensor processing, autonomous missions and built-in safety
functions. It combines dual IMUs, a pressure sensor and microSD flight logging, supports 2S to 8S
LiPo batteries and works with popular flight software including Betaflight, iNav and ArduPilot. Led
by CEO Dr Shian Lee, Alphaswift Industries develops drone technologies for logistics, agriculture and
emergency response.

## Automotive, FPGA and Space

Silicon X has built Malaysia's and Southeast Asia's first homegrown FPGA, a chip that can be
reprogrammed after it is made. It packs 30,000 logic elements into a 14nm chip and targets automotive
systems and intelligent motor control such as smart billboards and large LED screens. Led by Managing
Director Ng Meng Wah, Silicon X develops its chips and the software tools to use them entirely
in-house, and is extending its FPGAs to telecommunications, AI, healthcare and aerospace.

Infinecs Systems' Maxwell is an automotive low dropout regulator (LDO) that takes up to 40V input and
delivers a steady 3.3V or 5V output at up to 150mA, giving in-car electronics such as infotainment
screens and headlamps clean, stable power. Built-in protection against low voltage, short circuits
and overheating lets it operate from -40°C to 125°C, and it also suits telecommunications equipment,
battery management systems and motor drives. Infinecs Systems is led by CEO and co-founder Kalai
Selvan Subramaniam, with a team drawing on decades of experience at Intel and Motorola.

Weeroc Space's Conan is a power controller chip for efficient gallium nitride (GaN)-based power
conversion in satellites and other space systems, operating from 12 V to 138 V and managing up to
10 A. The radiation-tolerant IC features overcurrent, voltage drop and overheating protection,
redundant safety mechanisms, real-time voltage, current and temperature monitoring, and eight
independent heater controllers. Weeroc Space, a European semiconductor design company, runs its
design centre at the IC Design Park in Puchong; led by CEO Dr Salleh Ahmad, its Malaysian team
designs radiation-hardened chips for aerospace, satellite and scientific applications, supporting
projects involving ESA, NASA and Airbus Defense & Space.

## From Manufacturing to Design

The launch reflects the growing capability of Malaysian companies in front-end IC design, as the
country moves from "Made in Malaysia" to "Made by Malaysia".

> "The launch of these six new chips represent a new chapter exemplifying the prowess of Selangor's
> young engineers to be at the forefront of technological development."
> — YAB Dato' Seri Amirudin bin Shari, Dato' Menteri Besar Selangor

> "Malaysia has long been a key player in semiconductor manufacturing. The chips launched today show
> that Malaysian companies are also capable of high-value design work."
> — YB Akmal Nasrullah Mohd Nasir, Minister of Economy

Akmal Nasrullah added that this is in line with the National Semiconductor Strategy's aim of moving
the industry up the value chain, and that the priority now is to scale the capability by developing
more engineers, more companies and more homegrown intellectual property. Amirudin said the
achievements should propel a new generation to compete with Asia's best and brightest minds and
signal that Selangorians are no longer just consumers, but have the intellectual capacity to create
and design new technologies that power the digital world.

## Building the Ecosystem Behind the Chips

All six companies are part of the Malaysia Semiconductor IC Design Park, developed by Sidec and
described as Southeast Asia's largest IC design park. It spans IC Design Park 1 in Puchong and IC
Design Park 2 in Cyberjaya, which houses Southeast Asia's first Advanced Chip Testing Centre and the
Advanced Semiconductor Academy of Malaysia (ASEM).

ASEM has trained 850 engineers to date, with a 100% placement rate, and companies in the IC Design
Park have access to electronic design automation (EDA) tools from Cadence, Synopsys, Siemens EDA and
Keysight. Together, these strengthen Malaysia's front-end design capability alongside its
established manufacturing base.

> "The IC Design Park currently hosts over 400 engineers across 20 companies, and these six chips are
> a tangible outcome of that ecosystem."
> — Yong Kai Ping, Chief Executive Officer of Sidec

Yong said the focus is to provide IC design companies with the talent, design tools, funding linkages
and market access they need to grow, adding that Sidec looks forward to supporting more Malaysian
companies as they take their designs from concept to silicon.

## AiSEM@Selangor 2026

The launch took place on the third day of AiSEM@Selangor 2026, the AI & Semiconductor Summit, which
runs from 14 to 17 OCTOBER at the Kuala Lumpur Convention Centre and Grand Hyatt Kuala Lumpur under
the theme "Designed By Malaysia: Building a Globally Competitive AI Semiconductor Ecosystem". A key
highlight of this year's summit is a showcase of more than 20 robots.

The four-day summit also features an exhibition of more than 150 technology exhibitors, conference
sessions with 41 local and international speakers, and the Malaysia Semiconductor Recruitment Day,
where more than 50 employers are hiring fresh graduates and experienced professionals.

## About Sidec

The Selangor Information Technology & Digital Economy Corporation (Sidec) is a state GLC mandated to
drive the state's digital economy agenda. Since 2015, Sidec has run accelerator programmes supporting
470 companies that have raised RM216 million, and it manages the Malaysia Semiconductor IC Design
Park and AiSEM@Selangor, the state's AI and semiconductor summit.
